03Two corrections that change the numbers

“Live years” was counting parking pages — every single time

The buy list credited molke-vital-fasten.de with 16 years. Read properly, it has 6 real content years — the rest were parking templates counted as live pages. Across the next four checked, measured-vs-claimed came out 12.5 vs 12, 6 vs 9, 3 vs 15, 8 vs 9 — inflated every time, and capture counts understated every time (1,490 vs 345 on one).

And "owner verified dead" was wrong three times out of four. Treat that column in the 68-domain pool as unverified. Every figure in the table above is a re-measured one.

The most useful thing we learned, and it makes the next batch ten times faster. The killer is almost never spam — three of the last four were content-clean. It's the tail: whatever Google last scored before the domain went quiet. Parking feed, dead corporate error page, or health misinformation.

So the new first test is: read the last real page the domain ever served. If that's a parking template or an error page, kill it and move on. That one check would have dropped three of yesterday's four in ten minutes instead of hours.

On your Apotheke pick — you get one, and it's the safe one. kloster-apotheke-koeln.de is out: real pharmacy, named pharmacist, and its pages were network boilerplate.

But biochemische-hausapotheke.de survives and came back as the lowest owner risk in the entire set — because it was never a pharmacy. "Biochemische Hausapotheke" is the ordinary German term for the twelve-salt Schüßler home kit, and its Impressum declares a private individual with no business at all. Apotheke in the name, zero designation exposure.

04The publishing schedule

Per domain. The whole design is that no two of our sites ever look coordinated — the fleet that displaced us pushed 9 domains in 66–179 seconds, and that timing is the cheapest fingerprint there is to detect.

Cross-fleet rules

  • Never more than 4 domains publish in the same 24 hours, and never two in the same hour. Minimum 90 minutes apart, jittered ±40.
  • German business hours, never on a round hour, and each domain skips one random weekday a week. Real sites are irregular.
  • Two launches a day maximum — ten domains means five launch days spread over 10–12 days. Don't launch all six this week.
  • The same offer on four domains maximum, and those four must be the four with the most different histories — a shop, a food blog, a fasting site, a seniors' portal. Four different days, 48 hours apart, genuinely different page structure each time.
  • Offer pages only from week 5, and only once the domain has ~25 non-commercial pages indexed. A restored domain whose first ten pages are product reviews is a fresh affiliate shell wearing a costume — that's the 11-day-old profile that got re-scored on 25 July.
  • One sitemap submission per day across the whole fleet. Don't batch ten.

05What we never carry over

We restore the paths and the topics. All the German copy is new. Nothing identifying the previous operator is reproduced anywhere — not the name, address, phone, VAT or register number, not the Impressum, not photos or logos, not the trade name in a title, and not the text even where the text is good. That includes alt text and structured data.

Two specific ones: the Schüßler-salt book on biochemische-hausapotheke.de is a copyrighted work — we reuse the topic list, never a sentence. And the old practitioner directories don't come back in any form.

Each site gets its own Impressum naming your US company, its own privacy page, its own affiliate disclosure and its own contact page.
